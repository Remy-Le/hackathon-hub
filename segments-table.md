# Hackathon Hub — Segments at a Glance

*Compiled from `.agents/desires-fears-matrix.md`, `.agents/awareness-matrix.md`, `.agents/customer-research-icp.md`, `.agents/product-marketing.md`.*

> **Confidence note:** most of this is inferred from proxy sources (organizer guides, market structure, founder narrative) — not yet confirmed via direct interviews with real Hackathon Hub users. Treat as a strong first draft, and prioritize validating it with 5-10 real participant/organizer/sponsor conversations (ambassadors are the fastest warm sample).

| Dimension | Participants ("Hackers") | Organizers (uni / community / startup) | Sponsors / Corporate Recruiters | Ambassadors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Desires** | <ul><li>Find a hackathon truly worth it in one pass (location, language, prize, dates)</li><li>Prove/track skill level via the leaderboard</li><li>Build a portfolio/CV</li><li>Meet cofounders</li></ul> | <ul><li>A predictable flow of *qualified* participants who follow through to submission, not just registrants</li><li>A recurring sponsor pipeline instead of starting from zero each year</li><li>A low-effort submission/listing process</li></ul> | <ul><li>Reach a pool of qualified European technical talent</li><li>Convert a defensible share of participants into candidates/hires</li><li>A cost lower than a third-party recruiter (~20-25% of salary)</li></ul> | <ul><li>Be connected to a network bigger than their own region/community</li><li>Real recognition and perks for the role</li></ul> |
| **Fears** | <ul><li>Missing a great event without knowing it happened</li><li>Investing in an event that doesn't match their level</li><li>Betting on a "Phase 1" platform that could disappear or let listings go stale</li></ul> | <ul><li>Listings inflating registrations without cutting no-shows</li><li>Exposure reaching the wrong (too generic, not targeted) audience</li><li>Depending on a community platform with no guaranteed traffic quality</li></ul> | <ul><li>Paying for "visibility" with no proof of real impact</li><li>Being seen as a "deep-pocketed recruiter" that crowds out the community spirit</li><li>Investing in a platform with no clear pricing or case studies</li></ul> | <ul><li>Investing volunteer time in a still-"Phase 1" program with no clear return or guaranteed longevity</li></ul> |
| **Frustrations** | <ul><li>Info scattered across Luma, Devpost, LinkedIn, Discord, university sites</li><li>Dead pages and stale links</li><li>Having to cross-check 5+ sources for one decision</li><li>Global aggregators (Devpost, MLH) are weak on Europe</li></ul> | <ul><li>Promotion is a *sorting* problem, not a *reach* problem — generic messaging converts no one</li><li>30-50% no-show is a structural, accepted tax</li><li>Sourcing sponsors is a permanent, never-solved grind</li></ul> | <ul><li>Sponsorship ROI is nearly impossible to prove (~10% of participants ever meet a sponsor)</li><li>Real costs (travel, staff time) can double the nominal sponsorship</li><li>No comparable metrics across events</li></ul> | <ul><li>Their local community stays isolated from the rest of Europe, with no natural bridge to other regions/themes</li></ul> |
| **Dreams** | <ul><li>"Where I feel most alive" — the energy of all-nighters, strangers at 3 a.m. who become friends or cofounders</li><li>Building a reputation that's recognized beyond a single organizer (cross-event status)</li></ul> | <ul><li>An event that runs itself thanks to automatic pan-European distribution</li><li>Becoming a regional/thematic reference without depending solely on their own network</li></ul> | <ul><li>One clear overview of the whole European hackathon landscape through a single channel</li><li>A partnership that builds real brand affinity, not just a logo on a banner</li></ul> | <ul><li>Belonging to a recognized network of 90+ people in 25+ countries actively shaping the European hackathon scene</li></ul> |
| **How to target** | <ul><li>Unaware → sell the missed *experience* ("late nights that create friendships and cofounders — how many did you miss this year?"), not the product</li><li>Problem Aware → name the pain in their words ("scattered," "dead pages," "found out after it was already over")</li><li>Solution Aware → position the EU-aggregator category vs. Devpost ("5x more events for Europe")</li><li>Product Aware → lead with proof + reassurance (community-verified, no ads/no tracking, active ambassadors in 25+ countries) → CTA: free signup / AI search</li><li>Most Aware → simple recall + status ("You're #X on the leaderboard — climb higher," "new this month near you")</li></ul> | <ul><li>Reframe from reach to *sorting*: "You don't have a visibility problem, you have a right-audience problem"</li><li>Lead with the ambassador network as an active sourcing engine (sponsors + participants), not a passive directory — the most differentiated, evidence-backed wedge</li><li>Pitch the leaderboard as a B2B signal of participant intent/track record to reduce no-shows</li><li>CTA: one submission → automatic pan-EU distribution</li></ul> | <ul><li>Lead with the recruiting-funnel angle, not brand visibility — sponsors are ROI-skeptical ("90% never meet a future employee on-site")</li><li>Differentiate from MLH/direct sponsorship by pitching the ambassador network as a targeted sourcing layer, not a logo wall</li><li>Be transparent about lacking hard pricing/case studies today ("Phase 1") rather than dodging it — trust is the lever until real conversion data exists</li></ul> | <ul><li>Recruit on belonging + reach, not compensation: "join a 90+ person, 25+ country network shaping the European hackathon scene"</li><li>Be upfront about the Phase 1 stage so expectations on perks/support stay realistic</li></ul> |

## Strategic through-line

Every segment shares the same underlying fear: **wasting time or money on the wrong target** (participant at the wrong event, organizer reaching the wrong crowd, sponsor with unverifiable ROI). The mirrored desire across all of them is **precision, not volume** — the right match, not more noise. This lines up with the organizer insight already on file: *"it's not a reach problem, it's a sorting problem."*
