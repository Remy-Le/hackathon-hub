*Last updated: 2026-07-21*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Europe's central platform for discovering hackathons, competitions, and challenges — one place instead of scattered links across Luma, Devpost, LinkedIn, Discord, and university sites.

**What it does:** Hackathon Hub aggregates and lists hackathons, competitions, and challenges across Europe (currently 615+ events, 33+ countries, 144 live at time of crawl). Users can search, filter (time, country, city, type, language, duration, prize money, theme, sponsoring/travel), and browse via list, map, or calendar view. Logged-in users get AI-powered search that "understands synonyms, multiple languages and finds more relevant results." It also runs a cross-organizer hacker leaderboard, an event-submission flow for organizers, and an ambassador program for regional/community growth.

**Product category:** Event discovery / community platform for the hackathon & competition space (Europe-specific). Closest shelf: "Devpost, but for Europe and covering far more events."

**Product type:** Web platform / marketplace-style directory with a community layer (leaderboard, ambassadors) and an emerging B2B side (event hosts & sponsors).

**Business model:** Currently free to use, donation-supported (PayPal). Explicitly "Phase 1" — no monetization live yet. A B2B page exists for organizers/sponsors ("Create Event Host Account," sponsor inquiries) but no pricing is disclosed — this side looks pre-monetization too. *(Inferred: business model is still being built out — confirm with founder before publishing anything definitive.)*

## Target Audience
**Target companies/orgs (supply side — event hosts):** Universities, student/tech communities, startups, enterprises, and corporate innovation/HR teams running or sponsoring hackathons, competitions, and challenges in Europe.

**Target users (demand side — participants):** Individual hackathon participants/"hackers" across Europe — students, developers, builders — who want to find events to compete in.

**Decision-makers (B2B side):** Event organizers (community/university leads), corporate sponsors/HR & recruiting teams, innovation departments.

**Primary use case:** Finding relevant hackathons/competitions to enter (participant side); getting a hackathon or challenge in front of the European community (organizer/sponsor side).

**Jobs to be done:**
- Discover hackathons/challenges relevant to my location, skills, language, or prize interest without checking a dozen scattered sources.
- Prove/track my competitive standing across events via the leaderboard, to build credibility and unlock access (elite events, investor intros).
- (Organizers) Get a submitted event in front of the largest possible pool of European hackathon participants with minimal effort.
- (Sponsors) Reach a pan-European technical talent pool and connect with regional communities via ambassadors.

**Use cases / scenarios:**
- A student searches by country + "this month" + prize money to find a nearby hackathon.
- A top-ranked hacker gets invited to an "elite" event or a VC/investor mixer because of leaderboard standing.
- A university society submits its annual hackathon once and gets automatic categorization/distribution.
- A corporate sponsor wants regional visibility and works with local ambassadors instead of building EU-wide reach from scratch.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Hackathon and competition info in Europe is fragmented across many platforms and channels with "no central place that connects it all" — so participants miss events and regional communities stay siloed from each other.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Devpost-style directories are global-first and thin on European coverage (Hackathon Hub claims "5× more hackathons for Europe than Devpost does for the entire planet").
- Generic channels (Luma, LinkedIn, Discord, university pages, local meetup groups) each only cover a slice — nothing aggregates or normalizes them.
- No independent, cross-organizer way for participants to build a track record — rankings are locked to individual event/organizer silos.

**What it costs them:** Missed events ("dead pages," "communities I only heard about after the event was already over"), wasted time cross-checking many sources, isolated regional scenes that don't cross-pollinate or attract sponsors efficiently.

**Emotional tension:** FOMO on formative experiences ("the energy, the late nights, the strangers at 3 a.m. who become friends and cofounders") mixed with frustration at chaotic, dead-end discovery.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Devpost — global-first, broader category (all hackathons worldwide) but comparatively thin on Europe; Hackathon Hub leads with EU-specific depth as its wedge.

**Secondary:** Generic event/community tools used ad hoc for hackathon discovery — Luma, LinkedIn event pages, Discord servers, university websites, local meetup groups. Each solves discovery for one org/region but none aggregate.

**Indirect:** Word-of-mouth / personal networks — the informal way people currently find out about hackathons after the fact ("communities I only heard about after the event was already over"). Hackathon Hub's real competitor for many users may simply be "not knowing an event existed."

**Newly identified direct competitors (via customer research, 2026-07-21):** HackTrack EU (open-source EU hackathon listing), Hackalist, HackEvents — all solve the same "aggregate hackathons in one place" problem. Traction/usage unconfirmed; see `.agents/customer-research-icp.md` for detail. This weakens the "no one aggregates Europe" framing — differentiation should lean harder on EU coverage depth + community layer (leaderboard, ambassadors) + no-ads/no-tracking stance, not on being the only aggregator.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Europe-specific depth and coverage (615+ events, 33+ countries; claims 5x Devpost's European coverage).
- No ads, no tracking positioning — "No ads. No tracking. Just hackathons."
- Community-submitted and community-maintained listings ("authentic and credible") rather than purely organizer- or company-driven.
- Independent, cross-organizer hacker leaderboard — "the first leaderboard independent from any organizer" — with real unlocks (exclusive events, investor intros).
- Ambassador network (90+ people, 25+ countries) doing on-the-ground sourcing and partnership-building, not just a top-down directory.

**How we do it differently:** Instead of organizers manually promoting in their own bubble, Hackathon Hub centralizes submission + distribution, layers on AI search, and adds a community/status layer (leaderboard, ambassadors) that gives participants a reason to keep coming back beyond one-off event lookups.

**Why that's better:** Participants get comprehensive, low-noise discovery in one place; organizers get free, low-effort access to a much larger pan-European audience than their own network; the whole model compounds as ambassadors and community submissions grow coverage further.

**Why customers choose us:** Breadth of European coverage no single competitor matches, a values-forward "no ads/no tracking, community-first" stance that builds trust, and a leaderboard/status layer that gives power users an ongoing reason to engage (not just a one-time lookup tool).

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "How do I know listings are legit/complete if it's community-submitted?" | Frame around "authentic and credible" — community-sourced + ambassador-verified, contrasted with stale/dead links elsewhere. *(Needs a stronger, evidence-backed answer — consider adding moderation/QA detail.)* |
| "Is this just another Devpost clone?" | Lead with EU-specific depth (5x coverage claim) and the no-ads/no-tracking, non-corporate positioning as the wedge, not feature parity. |
| "What's the catch / how do you make money?" | Currently donation-supported, Phase 1, transparent about not having a monetization model yet — lean into transparency rather than dodging the question. |

**Anti-persona:** Organizers/sponsors looking for a heavily gated, premium, white-glove B2B product with paid guarantees today — the platform is still pre-monetization and community/volunteer-driven, so it's a poor fit for buyers expecting an established enterprise sales motion.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Frustration with fragmented, dead-end discovery (scattered links, outdated pages) and missing out on events after the fact.

**Pull:** A single place with far more European coverage than existing directories, plus a leaderboard/status layer and community (ambassador) access that generic listing sites don't offer.

**Habit:** Existing muscle memory of checking Luma/LinkedIn/Discord/uni pages/personal networks — habitual, low-effort even if incomplete.

**Anxiety:** Whether a community-submitted, pre-monetization, "Phase 1" platform will still be around or well-maintained long-term; whether listings are as complete/accurate as claimed.
